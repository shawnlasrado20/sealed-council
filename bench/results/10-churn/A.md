**Test first**

1. **A simpler first box.** New customers get 2–3 twenty-minute recipes, pre-portioned, with storage and cook cards. First-box loss (18%) is twice later monthly churn (9%). Cooking friction is highest before the habit exists. “Too expensive” often means the effort did not justify the price.

2. **Skip/pause and a smaller box instead of cancel.** Put one-tap skip and a 2-person / 3-meal option on the post-box and pre-renewal screens. Treats “too expensive” and “too much cooking” as a fit problem, not a product reject.

**How to measure**

Run a holdout RCT on new cohorts only, 4–6 weeks.

- **Primary:** first-box → second-box conversion.
- **Secondary:** month-2 retention, skip-vs-cancel ratio, contribution margin per subscriber, recipe completion, spoilage tickets.
- **Guardrails:** reorder rate, NPS, CAC payback, share choosing the cheaper SKU.

**Avoid**

- Cutting list price for everyone. Exit surveys overweight price; you train discount-seeking and damage unit economics.
- Shipping a full menu or ops overhaul at once. You will not know what moved the 18%.
- Leading with a freshness overhaul unless spoilage tickets already spike. It is 15% stated, slower to test, and a hygiene fix to monitor—not the first A/B.
