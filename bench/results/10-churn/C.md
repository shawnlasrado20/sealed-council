## Test 1: a smaller, cheaper plan offered at signup and at cancel

For example, 2 meals instead of 3–4, or 2 servings instead of 4. This one change speaks to "too expensive" (40%) and "food went bad" (15%), so it covers 55% of stated exit reasons. It lowers what customers pay each week without cutting your price per meal, and it means less food sitting in the fridge.

## Test 2: a "15-minute meals" track as the default for new customers

New customers would see the easiest recipes first: fewer steps, pre-chopped ingredients, one pan. This addresses "too much cooking" (30%). It also protects the first box, which matters because that is where the 18% leak is, and that leak is twice the monthly churn.

## How to measure

- **Design:** randomized A/B test at signup, with each new cohort split into control and variant. Run the two tests separately, or as a 2x2 if you have enough traffic, so you can tell which change did what.
- **Primary metric:** retention to box 2 and box 4, plus 90-day contribution margin per customer. Track margin, not just headcount, because a cheaper plan can keep more customers while earning less from each.
- **Secondary metrics:** skip rate, plan-size changes, and how many customers on the small plan later move up.
- **Guardrails:** gross margin per box, food cost per meal, recipe ratings and complaint rate.
- **Segmentation:** break results down by acquisition channel. Customers who came in through a discount usually churn very differently from the rest.
- **Duration:** let cohorts mature for at least 8–12 weeks before calling a result. Pre-compute the sample size you need to detect a 2–3 point change in box-2 retention.

## What to avoid

- **Blanket price cuts or bigger first-box discounts.** They attract deal-seekers, make first-box churn worse and erode margin. "Too expensive" often means the box doesn't feel worth the price, not that the price is literally too high.
- **Taking exit surveys at face value.** They capture the reasons people state, not necessarily the real ones. Check them against behavior, such as whether customers actually cooked the meals or skipped weeks first.
- **Making cancellation harder.** Dark patterns damage trust and invite regulatory trouble. Offering "pause" or "downsize" as an alternative at cancel is fine.
- **Shipping several changes at once.** If you change many things together, you can't tell which one moved the numbers.
