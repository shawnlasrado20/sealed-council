RANK: B > A

B is the stronger analysis. It keeps price fixed in the first-box test, so the convenience effect isn't confounded with a cheaper offer. It ties "food went bad" to box size, which addresses spoilage without a separate freshness project. B's main measure for the right-sizing test is 90-day contribution margin per acquired customer. It also warns that skips can just push churn later. Its rigor points are sound: separate or factorial randomization, a sample size set in advance, and following cohorts long enough.

A is tighter and well organized. It makes good use of the 18% vs 9% gap and clearly explains why spoilage comes later. But it changes several things at once in the first box, and 4 to 6 weeks may be too short to see later churn. It also never flags that skips can hide churn.

B probably goes past the 300-word limit, which counts against it. Its better measurement thinking still outweighs A's conciseness.
