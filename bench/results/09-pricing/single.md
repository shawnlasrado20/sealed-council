**Launch with:** $12/month individual or $99/year, plus a $49/month team plan (up to 5 seats) with shared doc workspace/branding. Skip the $49 one-time license and usage-based pricing for now.

**Why:**
- **The 12 who asked to pay are your price discovery.** Before you set anything, email all 12 and ask: "What would you pay, and would you expense it?" If more than half say they'd expense it, you're priced too low at $8.
- **$8 is too cheap for a doc tool.** People save hours per doc with it. $8 signals "toy" and leaves no margin for support. $12 to $15 is still an easy impulse buy.
- **A one-time license kills recurring revenue.** For a solo dev, MRR is what lets you keep going. It also undervalues the tool for heavy users. If people ask, offer a "lifetime" deal later as a limited cash injection.
- **Usage-based pricing adds friction too early.** Per-recording metering makes users hesitate before each use, which hurts habit formation. Revisit it if compute costs (transcription, LLM calls) become significant.
- **Team is where the real money is.** Docs get shared. The team tier should gate collaboration features, not core usage.

**Free tier:** Keep it, but cap it at something like 5 docs/month, with export watermarks or no custom branding. You need a reason to upgrade that isn't arbitrary.

**What to test first (in order):**
1. **Willingness to pay (this week):** Send the 12 a direct Stripe checkout link at $12/month. Measure conversion. If 6 or more convert, raise the price for the next cohort.
2. **Price point (next 4 weeks):** Show new signups $12 vs $19 on the pricing page. With 400 users the data will be noisy, so look for big gaps, not statistical significance.
3. **Team demand:** Count how many paying users invite someone or ask about sharing. If that's under 10%, delay building team features.

Re-evaluate at 50 paying users.
