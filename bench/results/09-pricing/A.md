I’d launch **one paid plan at $8/month for individuals**, cancel anytime, with a small free allowance to try the full workflow.

That’s a starting hypothesis: 12 people asking to pay shows interest, but says little about their budget or whether they need this every month.

**First test: ask those 12 to buy.**

1. Within a week, offer the existing tool at $8/month through a real checkout. Keep the offer identical for everyone. Ask what they last documented, how often they do it, and who pays for their tools.
2. Set a practical first hurdle: **at least 4 purchases within seven days**. This is a decision rule, not a statistically reliable conversion estimate. If fewer buy, interview the nonbuyers before changing the price.
3. For buyers, track successful document creation, use in separate weeks, second-month renewal, and processing costs. Four purchases followed by four cancellations suggests a different business from four recurring users.

**What would change my choice:**

- **$49 one-time:** test next if users describe occasional projects and resist subscriptions. Only offer it if ongoing processing costs are negligible or paid through their own API keys; define which version and updates it includes.
- **$29/month team:** introduce when buyers demonstrate shared workflows and a company budget. Don’t build collaboration features just to justify a tier.
- **Usage-based:** consider if processing costs vary substantially with recording length. First measure those costs; avoid surprising bills.

Even all 12 paying $8 yields only **$96 MRR**. The immediate goal is proving repeated paid use.

Skipped competitor research and a four-way pricing experiment. Main risk: usage frequency and processing costs are still unknown.
